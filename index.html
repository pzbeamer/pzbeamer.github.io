About

I use mathematics to study how the shape of biological systems influences their biological function. In that vein, my interests span applied mathematics and computational biology. Mathematically, I specialize in topological data analysis (TDA) and optimal transport (OT). These computational techniques characterize the shape (i.e. the geometry and topology) of complex real-world data. I am also interested in data-driven mathematical modeling and machine learning, especially at their intersection with TDA and OT. Biologically, I primarily study biological problems involving complex spatial structure, including molecular structure prediction and spatial transcriptomics data analysis. Applying TDA and OT, I harness a system's geometric/topological structure to predict its biological function.

Currently, I am in my fourth year of my Biomathematics PhD at NC State, advised by Dr. Zixuan Cang. Before arriving at NC State in 2022, I completed my bachelor's degree in mathematics and philosophy at the University of Maryland.
